How to Use RedFi

What each input does, and what changes when you edit it

Valuation Studio

Estimates what a company is worth today, based on projecting its future cash flow and discounting it back to the present.

STEPS

  1. Name the company — just a label, doesn't affect the math.
  2. Set base revenue — the company's current annual revenue, in millions.
  3. Set growth rates for Years 1–5 — how fast revenue grows each year. It's normal for this to decline over time as a company matures.
  4. Set EBIT margin for Year 1 and Year 5 — operating profit margin at the start and end; the years between are interpolated automatically.
  5. Set D&A, capex, and ΔNWC — reinvestment costs as a % of revenue, used to convert profit into actual cash.
  6. Set the tax rate — applied to operating profit.
  7. Set WACC — the discount rate; higher WACC means future cash is worth less today.
  8. Set terminal growth — how fast the company grows forever after Year 5. Keep this small (2–3%) — it drives a large share of the result.
  9. Set net debt and shares outstanding — used to convert total company value into a per-share price.

WHAT UPDATES AS YOU TYPE

  • Implied share price — the headline number at the top of the page.
  • FCF table — shows how each year's cash flow is built, from revenue down to free cash flow.
  • Value bridge — walks from discounted cash flows to enterprise value, then to equity value.
  • Sensitivity grid — shows how the share price shifts if your WACC or growth assumptions are off.

Deal Desk

Tests whether buying a company outright, at a given price, would help or hurt the acquirer's earnings per share. This models acquiring the whole company — not buying shares as a market investor.

STEPS

  1. Enter the acquirer's details — its current share price, share count, and net income.
  2. Enter the target's current market price — used to calculate the premium being paid.
  3. Enter the offer price — what the acquirer is actually paying per share to buy out every target shareholder. You can plug in a Valuation Studio number here if you want to test that price.
  4. Enter the target's share count and net income.
  5. Set the cash/stock mix — how the acquirer is paying: 0% = all cash, 100% = all new stock issued.
  6. Set the cost of cash used — the interest rate or opportunity cost tied to the cash portion.
  7. Set the tax rate and expected synergies — annual cost savings or revenue gains expected from combining the two companies.

WHAT UPDATES AS YOU TYPE

  • Accretion / (dilution) % — the headline number. Green means the deal helps the acquirer's EPS, red means it hurts it.
  • Deal terms — total deal value, premium paid, and the cash/stock split in dollars.
  • Net income bridge — shows exactly how the combined company's net income is built.
  • EPS comparison — standalone EPS vs. pro forma EPS, side by side.
